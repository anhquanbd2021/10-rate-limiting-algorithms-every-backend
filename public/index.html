Rate Limiter Lab · deterministic clock

Ten names on the listicle. Four actual mechanisms.

Fire the same request stream at every algorithm and the differences stop being vocabulary: fixed window admits 2× the limit at a boundary, sliding variants pay memory for correctness, token bucket separates rate from burst, leaky bucket emits jitter-free output, and the concurrency limit catches the attack every per-second limiter misses.

Safe educational demo. Everything runs in your browser on an injected clock — no network calls, no Redis, no real traffic shaping. It's a model of the mechanics, not infrastructure.

Boundary lab

Fixed window admits 2× at the edge

Ten requests packed against a window rollover — five just before t=1000ms, five just after. Fixed window sees two legal windows; the sliding variants see one rolling span with ten requests in it. Same stream, three verdicts.

    Buckets lab

    Token bursts. Leaky meters.

    Same input, same admit count — different output. The token bucket had capacity banked during the idle period, so the whole burst passes in 70ms. The leaky bucket accepts the same eight into its queue and emits at exactly 500ms intervals. Rate and burst are different dials; a constant drain is a different product.

    Concurrency lab

    The axis rate limiters can't see

    Eleven requests over ~5 seconds — roughly 2/s. A generous fixed window of 10/s admits all of them. But six requests each hold their slot for five seconds, so the fourth-and-last in-flight slot is gone: four legitimate short requests are rejected while the rate limiter sees nothing wrong.

      Deployment & clients lab

      Where the "algorithms" stop being algorithms

      Distributed counter: two nodes with local counters admit 2× the quota unless the counter is shared and atomic. Per-user quota: a policy — any mechanism keyed by identity — that stops a noisy neighbor. Backoff: client discipline — eight rejected clients retrying on the same 200ms tick vs exponential + jitter.
